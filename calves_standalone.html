💪 Calves
Calf Session
Footwork & Ankle
👟
Why calves matter in martial arts: Your calves and ankles are the base of all footwork. The push-off power in every step, every pivot, every explosive lateral movement comes from here. Strong calves also improve ankle stability — reducing the most common lower body injury in martial arts.
💪 Exercise 1 — Standing Calf Raises
Standing Calf Raises
○
4 Sets
25 reps
20 sec rest
Step edge for full range
Full range: Heel drops below the step at the bottom — this stretch is essential for growth
Rise high: Push up onto the balls of your feet as high as possible — full contraction
Slow negative: 3 seconds lowering — calves grow from the stretch, not just the contraction
High reps work: Calves are used to walking all day — they need high volume to grow
Martial arts connection: The calf raise mimics the push-off in every step and pivot — the foundation of boxing footwork and kicking power

💪 Exercise 2 — Single Leg Calf Raises
Single Leg Calf Raises
○
3 Sets each leg
15 reps
20 sec rest
One leg only: Hold onto something for balance — all your bodyweight on one calf
Much harder: Single leg doubles the load — reduce reps if needed
Fixes imbalances: Reveals if one calf is weaker than the other — common in fighters who favor one side
Martial arts connection: Single leg push-off strength — the exact force used when pivoting for a punch or pushing off to change levels

💪 Exercise 3 — Jump Rope or Jumping Jacks
Jump Rope
○
3 Sets
2 Minutes each
30 sec rest
Calf endurance
Stay on toes: Land on the balls of your feet — not flat footed. This is calf training, not cardio
Light and quick: Small quick jumps — efficiency over height
No rope? Jumping jacks on your toes — same calf engagement
Martial arts connection: Calf and ankle endurance — what keeps fighters light on their feet in late rounds when others go flat-footed
Round Timer
2:00
💪 Exercise 4 — Ankle Circles
Ankle Mobility Circles
○
2 Sets each ankle
20 circles each direction
10 sec rest
Seated or standing: Lift foot off floor and rotate ankle in full circles — clockwise then counter
Full range: Make the biggest circle you can — push to the edges of your range
Martial arts connection: Ankle mobility prevents ankle sprains and improves kicking mechanics — often the difference between a kick that lands and one that gets caught
