BJJ Hip Escape — Complete Guide
The single most important movement in BJJ
Starting position: Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the floor
Frame and push: Turn onto one shoulder, plant the opposite foot, and push your hips away in a sweeping arc
Knee follows hip: As your hip shoots back, bring your knee toward your chest — this creates the frame that keeps opponents out
Why it matters: Hip escapes are how you create space from any bottom position — guard, mount, side control. Everything starts here
Reset and repeat: Return to center, then shrimp to the other side

Technical Stand-Up — Step by Step
How to safely get up when someone is in front of you
From seated: Sit on the ground, post one hand behind you, and bring the opposite foot flat under your hips
Stand on one leg: Push through the planted foot to rise up, keeping your other leg bent in front as a guard/kick option
Eyes forward: In live application you'd be facing an opponent — always keep your front leg ready to defend
Why it matters: This is THE way to stand up safely in a fight or grappling exchange without exposing your back or legs to attack

Forward Shoulder Roll — Breakfall Guide
Roll across the shoulder, never the head or neck
Tuck your chin: Look toward your hip, not down — your chin tucks to your chest to protect your neck
Roll across the shoulder diagonally: You roll diagonally across your back from one shoulder to the opposite hip — never straight over the spine or head
Stay rounded: Keep your body curled like a ball throughout the roll
Why it matters: Falling and rolling safely prevents injury during throws, sweeps and scrambles — this is a core safety skill

🥋 Drill 4 — Backward Shoulder Rolls
Backward Shoulder Rolls
○
2 Sets
5 each side
30 sec rest
Sit and rock: Start seated, rock backward onto one shoulder, tucking your chin away from that side
Roll over the shoulder diagonally: Same diagonal path as forward rolls but in reverse — never roll straight onto your head or neck
Recover to your feet or knees: Use the momentum to come up into a stable position
Why it matters: Backward rolls develop the spatial awareness and recovery skills used when scrambling or being swept
