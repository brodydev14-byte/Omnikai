Upa Bridge Escape — Mount Escape Fundamentals
Explosive hip bridge to escape mount
Setup: Lie on your back, plant both feet flat near your hips, trap one arm across your chest like defending mount
Explosive bridge: Drive your hips straight up as high and fast as possible — full extension
Roll to the side: As your hips peak, roll over your shoulder toward the trapped-arm side — this is what dumps an opponent off in mount
Why it matters: The upa is the primary explosive escape from mount — timing and hip power are everything

Sprawl — Takedown Defense Basics
The first line of defense against any shot
Start standing: From your stance, explosively drop your hips back and down while kicking both legs back
Chest to floor: Your hips drop low while your chest comes forward and down — heavy on top of an imaginary opponent's shoulders
Recover fast: Snap your legs back under you and return to standing immediately — speed of recovery matters as much as the sprawl itself
Why it matters: Sprawling is your first response to any takedown attempt — drilling it builds the reflex

Sit-Out Drill — Turtle Escape Basics
Wrestling and BJJ staple movement
From all fours: Start on hands and knees — this mimics turtle position
Sit through: Sweep one leg underneath your body and sit out to that side, ending up facing the opposite direction with hips low
Drive with the hips: The power comes from rotating your hips through, not just moving your legs
Alternate sides: Sit out one way, return to base, then sit out the other way
Why it matters: Sit-outs are the foundation of escaping turtle position and back takes — extremely common in both BJJ and wrestling
