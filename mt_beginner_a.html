Muay Thai Teep — Complete Guide
The most important kick to learn first
Lead leg teep: Thrust your lead leg forward — push through with the ball of your foot, not your toes
Hip drives it: The power comes from driving your hip forward as you extend — not just lifting your leg
Purpose: The teep controls distance — push opponents back, create space, interrupt their rhythm
Retract fast: Pull your leg back quickly after contact — don't leave it extended
Guard stays up: Both hands up at all times even when kicking

Muay Thai Roundhouse — Proper Technique
Hip rotation is everything — watch before drilling
Strike with the SHIN: Not the foot — the shin is your weapon in Muay Thai. Aim to connect with the lower third of your shin
Pivot your base foot: As you kick, pivot on the ball of your standing foot — this rotates your hip through the kick
Hip rotation drives power: The kick starts from your hip — rotate your whole body into it, not just your leg
Swing your arm: Drop your lead arm as you kick — this naturally rotates your hip further and adds power
Don't chamber first: Muay Thai kicks don't chamber like karate — they swing through in one fluid motion

Set up the kick: The jab and cross are distractions — they open the guard for the kick
Don't telegraph: Throw the punches with real intent — if you fake them your opponent will see the kick coming
Step in for the kick: After the cross your weight is forward — use that momentum to pivot into the roundhouse
Return to stance: After every combo reset your Muay Thai stance immediately
