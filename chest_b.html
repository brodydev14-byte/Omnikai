Chest Session B
Dumbbell
🥊
Dumbbell chest for fighters: Dumbbells give a greater range of motion than barbells — the stretch at the bottom of a fly or press directly mimics the chest engagement in hooks and wide punches. Each arm works independently, fixing muscle imbalances common in fighters.

Flat Dumbbell Press — Form Guide
Setup, elbow angle and range of motion
Setup: Lie flat, dumbbells at chest level, elbows at 45-75 degrees from your body
Full range: Lower dumbbells until you feel a good stretch in the chest — don't cut the range short
Press and squeeze: At the top, squeeze your chest like you're trying to crush something between your pecs
Feet flat: Keep feet flat on the floor — don't arch excessively
Martial arts connection: Middle chest mass and pushing strength — the power behind straight punches and pushing opponents away

Upper chest
Incline angle: Set bench to 30-45 degrees — going higher than 45 starts hitting shoulders more than chest
Go slightly lighter: Upper chest is generally weaker — use 10-20% less weight than flat press
Same press pattern: Same motion as flat but angled — elbows at 45-75 degrees
Martial arts connection: Upper chest fullness and strength helps with underhooks, clinch control and driving opponents into the cage or mat

Go lighter!
▶️
Dumbbell Flyes — Form & Safety
Going too heavy on flyes causes shoulder injuries
Light weight: Flyes are an isolation exercise — use significantly less than your press weight
Slight bend: Keep a slight bend in the elbows throughout — never fully straight arms
Arc motion: Think of hugging a big tree — wide arc down and back up
Feel the stretch: The stretch at the bottom is the point — don't cut the range short
Martial arts connection: Chest stretch and contraction range — builds the full punch extension and retraction strength
