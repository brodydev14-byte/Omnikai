Why wrap your hands: Hand wraps protect the small bones and tendons in your hands and wrists, stabilize your wrist joint, and absorb impact. Never hit pads or a bag without them.
▶️
How to Wrap Your Hands — Full Tutorial
Step by step video guide
1
Secure the Thumb Loop
Slide your thumb through the loop at the end of the wrap. Wrap around your wrist 2-3 times for support, starting from the outside of your wrist.
2
Cover the Hand
Bring the wrap diagonally across the back of your hand to the base of your fingers. Wrap across your knuckles 2-3 times for padding — this is your main impact zone.
3
Wrap Each Finger
Weave the wrap between each finger at the base, going around the thumb once for extra support. This keeps your fingers from spreading on impact.
4
Reinforce the Knuckles
Return to the knuckles for 2-3 more passes — this is the most important padding layer. Make a fist while wrapping to ensure it's not too tight when you punch.
5
Finish at the Wrist
Wrap any remaining length around the wrist and secure with the velcro closure. Check that you can make a full fist and your wrist feels supported but not numb.

The gi and belt aren't just clothing — they carry centuries of tradition. Wearing them correctly and tying your belt properly is a sign of respect for the art, your training partners, and yourself.
▶️
How to Tie a Gi Belt — Square Knot
The standard belt knot used across BJJ, Judo & Karate
1
Find Your Center
Place the middle of the belt against your stomach. Wrap both ends around your back and bring them forward to the front.
2
Cross and Wrap Again
Cross the ends and wrap around your waist a second time so both ends are even in length at the front.
3
Tie the Square Knot
Cross the right end over the left and tuck it under all the wraps, pulling it through. Then cross that same end over again and tuck it through the loop to form a flat square knot — not a bow.
4
Even It Out
Pull both ends to tighten and adjust so they hang evenly. A properly tied belt stays flat and secure through an entire training session, even during scrambles.
🙏 Gi Etiquette
👘
Left side over right: When closing your gi jacket, the left side of the gi always goes on top of the right side. This convention is shared across BJJ, Judo, and Karate. Wrapping it the opposite way (right over left) is how garments are traditionally arranged for the deceased in many Asian cultures — getting it backwards is considered deeply disrespectful.
🧼
Always train clean: Wash your gi after every session. A clean gi is a basic sign of respect to your training partners.
🚫
Never step on the belt: Your belt represents your rank and training journey — treat it with care, don't toss it on the ground.
