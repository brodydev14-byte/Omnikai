Weighted Russian Twist — Form
Watch how to hold the plate correctly
Legs up and together: Same as bodyweight version — lift both legs off the floor and keep them together
Hold the plate: Grip it with both hands, touch the floor on each side — left and right is one rep
Control the weight: Don't let the plate swing — YOU control it, it doesn't control you
Go to near failure: Push through the burn — stop when form starts to break not when it gets uncomfortable
Progressive overload: Add 2.5-5lbs when you can do 20+ reps with good form — that's how you keep growing
Martial arts connection: The weighted rotation directly builds the power behind hooks, body shots and throwing in grappling arts

Hanging Leg Raises — Proper Form
Control the swing — no momentum
Grip: Dead hang from the pull up bar, shoulder-width grip, arms fully extended
No swinging: Control the movement — if you're swinging use momentum which takes the work off your core
Legs together: Keep legs together throughout — harder but more effective
Lower slowly: 3 seconds down — the lowering phase is where half the work happens
Progression: Bent knees first → straight legs → toes to bar as you get stronger
Martial arts connection: The strongest core exercise for guard retention in BJJ and high kick flexibility in striking arts

Ab Wheel — Form & Common Mistakes
The hardest core exercise — start from knees
Start from knees: Kneel on the floor, roll out slowly until your body is nearly parallel to the floor
Brace hard: Your core must be extremely tight — this is the most demanding core exercise there is
Don't collapse: If your lower back collapses stop immediately — work on planks first to build the base strength
Slow roll out: 3 seconds out, pause, 3 seconds back — never rush this movement
Martial arts connection: Full core engagement from hips to shoulders — the kind of core strength that makes you immovable on the ground

💪 Exercise 4 — Cable Rotation
Cable Rotation
○
3 Sets each side
12 reps
30 sec rest
Cable Machine
Setup: Stand sideways to the cable machine, handle at chest height, arms extended
Rotate from hips: The power comes from rotating your hips and torso — not just pulling with your arms
Stay tall: Don't lean or collapse — maintain upright posture throughout the rotation
Control back: Resist the cable as it pulls you back — don't just let it snap you to the start
Martial arts connection: This is the most direct simulation of punch and kick power generation — the same hip rotation, just loaded
