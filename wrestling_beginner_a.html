Wrestling is the backbone of MMA: Every elite MMA fighter has a strong wrestling base. The ability to control where the fight happens — standing or on the ground — is the single biggest advantage in combat sports. Solo drilling builds the reflexes that make this automatic.

🤼 Drill 1 — Wrestling Stance & Movement
Wrestling Stance & Footwork
○
3 Minutes
Movement only
▶️
Wrestling Stance & Basic Footwork
Different from boxing — lower and wider
Low base: Knees bent, hips low — much lower than a boxing stance. Your hips should feel like you're about to sit down
Staggered feet: One foot forward, weight even on both feet — ready to shoot or sprawl in any direction
Hands forward: Hands up and forward — ready to underhook, post or tie up
Circle and pressure: Move around in your stance — circle left, circle right, pressure forward and pull back

🤼 Drill 2 — Level Changes
Level Changes
○
3 Sets
10 reps
20 sec rest
Drop your level: From your stance, drop your hips straight down by bending your knees deeper — don't lean forward
Stand back up: Drive back up through your heels to full stance height
Stay square: Your shoulders stay square and your back stays relatively upright — this isn't a forward lean, it's a vertical drop
Why it matters: Level changes are the setup for EVERY takedown — you drop your level to get under your opponent's center of gravity. Without a sharp level change, shots are easy to defend

🤼 Drill 3 — Penetration Step (The Shot)
Penetration Step
○
3 Sets
8 each side
30 sec rest
▶️
Wrestling Penetration Step — Solo Drill
The foundation of every takedown
From your stance: Drop your level first, then drive your lead knee toward the mat between your opponent's feet
Knee to the mat: Your lead knee drives forward and down — this is the "shot" position
Back knee up: Your trailing knee comes up behind you — you're in a lunge position with lead knee on the mat
Head on the outside: Head goes to the outside of the imaginary opponent — not straight into their chest
Drive through: Once in shot position, drive your hips through by standing up — this is the finish of the double leg
Why it matters: This is THE fundamental wrestling movement. Every takedown in wrestling, BJJ and MMA starts with some variation of this step

🤼 Drill 4 — Single Leg Setup
Single Leg Entry
○
3 Sets
8 each side
30 sec rest
Same setup as double leg: Level change, penetration step — but this time you only grab one leg
Inside leg: Your lead leg drives to the inside of the imaginary opponent's lead leg — scoop under it
Head outside: Head goes to the outside of their hip — controls their balance
Why it matters: The single leg is often easier to hit than the double leg because it requires less penetration — knowing both gives you more options in live situations
